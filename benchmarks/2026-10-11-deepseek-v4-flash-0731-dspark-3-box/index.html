DeepSeek's own speed helper. On three boxes, it costs 24–32%.

DSpark is DeepSeek's official draft model for V4-Flash-0731: a small model that guesses 5 words ahead from the big model's own state, and the big model checks the guesses. On one machine it is reported to nearly double speed. Split across three boxes, every guess-and-check round has to cross the links between them, and only half the guesses were kept, so it came out slower.

Plain vs. DSpark, same three boxes, same test

The Hive Benchmarking Stack (llama-benchy 0.4.0): prompts of 512 and 2,048 tokens, 128-token answers, with no history and with 4,096 tokens of history. Each test runs 3 times. Both runs used the same model file, engine, boxes and flags; the only change is the DSpark drafter on the head.

24–32% slower at every size. Half the guesses were rejected, and each round trip crosses the box-to-box links.

What this tells us

Speculative decoding only pays when guesses are cheap to check. On this cluster, GLM-5.3-Flash's built-in MTP layer (71% kept) made answers 23–37% faster, while DSpark (51% kept) made DeepSeek slower. Next we are testing DSpark on just the two AMD boxes over the faster USB4 cable.

Draft acceptance is summed over every logged request, including warm-ups. DSpark drafts up to 3 tokens per step (the recommended setting for this drafter).
