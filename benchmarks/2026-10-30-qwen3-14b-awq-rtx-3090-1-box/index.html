One RTX 3090. 32 conversations at once. Up to 779 tokens a second.

Qwen3-14B in 4-bit AWQ, served by vLLM on a single 24 GB RTX 3090 held to 200 W. One conversation gets 46 tokens a second. Thirty-two at once push the card to a 779 tok/s peak, and every one of the 246 requests came back clean.

Same test, three crowd sizes, two depths

The Hive Benchmarking Stack (llama-benchy 0.4.0): a 512-token prompt and a 128-token answer, sent by 1, 8 or 32 conversations at once, with no history and with 4,096 tokens of history. Each test runs 3 times; the bar is the mean. Caching is off, so every request reads its whole prompt.

Temperatures and power during the run

Sampled every 15 seconds for the whole 12.5-minute run. The GPU climbed from 90 °F at idle to 144 °F in two minutes. The fans woke there, and it held 145–151 °F to the end at a steady 199 W.

red line = 185 °F (85 °C) automatic stop armed for this run

Why the card is held back

This card is capped at 200 W of its 350 W, and its core is locked at 1,200 MHz. At stock boost, this particular card hangs the moment a heavy load hits it cold. Locked, it took everything this test threw at it.

Room on the card, measured with the model loaded

vLLM claims 85% of the 24 GB up front. The 4-bit weights take 9.4 GB of it. Nearly everything else becomes cache for the conversations, which is how 32 of them fit at once.

With 4K of history, 32 conversations need more cache than the card holds, so vLLM queues some while others finish. That is the 60-second wait in the last chart.
